What the columns mean

Native speakers
People who speak the language as a first language, rounded. Figures without an asterisk are Ethnologue’s 2026 counts. Figures with one are our own rough estimates, there to give a sense of scale and nothing more.
Voice
The speech engine picked for a word’s audio. We name it because you are entitled to know whose voice you will hear. Word audio is not switched on in the app yet, so every voice here is tested, not live.
Audio follows the IPA
A voice normally guesses a pronunciation from the spelling, and for a word like read or record it can guess the wrong one. Here the voice is given the same IPA you see on the page. “Sounds and stress” means we heard it say those sounds with the stress where the IPA marks it. “Sounds” means the sounds follow and the stress was not obeyed or not judged. “Reads the spelling only” means no engine we tried follows IPA for that language, so the audio and the transcription can disagree.
Word forms
Recognising an inflected form as its dictionary word, so that ging leads to gehen. It runs today on the books we publish and is tested for the other languages listed.
Frequency
How common a word is, with the corpus it was counted in.
Dictionary entries
A stored entry with every sense of a word and a level for each one. This is the work in progress the rest of the table waits on.

How the pronunciation column was tested: each voice was sent a made-up word whose IPA spelled a different word than its text. A voice that says the IPA’s word follows the IPA; a voice that says the text does not. The stress was then moved from syllable to syllable and listened to. An engine accepting the request counted for nothing; only what was heard did.
